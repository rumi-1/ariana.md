**Balkh** is a city in northern [[Afghanistan]]. It has a long history, and many accounts describe it as one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Central Asia. Its ancient name, in Greek sources, was **Bactra**, and the surrounding region was known as **Bactria**.

## Ancient period

Balkh was the capital of Bactria, a rich region that was part of the Achaemenid Persian empire and later of the empires that followed Alexander the Great. Its position on the trade routes between the Iranian plateau, India, and China made it prosperous.

## Religious tradition

Balkh is traditionally associated with [[Zoroaster]], the prophet of Zoroastrianism, though the historical basis of that association is debated. The city was also an important centre of Buddhism during the period of Kushan rule.

## Islamic period

After the Arab conquest, Balkh became a major centre of learning. Medieval writers called it *Umm al-Bilad*, "mother of cities", a phrase that reflects its standing. Scholars and poets were active there, and it was a key city of [[Khorasan]].

The Mongol conquest in the early 13th century devastated the city, and it never recovered its earlier importance.

## Today

The modern town near the ruins is a provincial centre in northern Afghanistan. The surrounding area is known for its archaeological sites and for the old shrines and mosques associated with the city's history.
