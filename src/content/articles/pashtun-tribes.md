This page exists to make a large, genuinely complicated subject navigable, not to settle it. It sets out the traditional shape of [[Pashtuns|Pashtun]] tribal genealogy and links down into the confederacies and tribes, most of which do not have their own article yet — follow the links below as a to-do list, not a finished tree.

## How the genealogy is told

Pashtun tribal tradition traces nearly all tribes back to a single ancestor, Qais Abdur Rashid, said to have met the Prophet Muhammad and adopted Islam. His descendants are grouped into several major branches, which in turn divide into confederacies, tribes, clans, and sub-clans.

Two cautions matter here. First, these genealogies are oral and traditional; they encode real political alliances and migrations, but they are not a documentary record, and different sources group the same tribes differently. Second, "tribe" itself is a loose term in this context — the same word is used for groupings of very different size and political weight.

## The major confederacies

- [[Durrani]] (formerly Abdali) — produced the Durrani dynasty that founded the modern Afghan state in 1747.
- [[Ghilzai]] — a large confederacy of eastern and southeastern Afghanistan, including the Hotak dynasty that briefly ruled from Kandahar and Isfahan in the early 18th century.
- Karlani (or Karlanri) — includes well-known tribes of the Durand Line region such as Afridi, Khattak, Mehsud, and Wazir. No article yet.
- Sarbani — a large branch that, in most tellings, includes the Durrani and other tribes further east. No article yet.
- Gharghashti — a smaller branch. No article yet.
