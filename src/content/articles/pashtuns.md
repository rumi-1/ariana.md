This page is a hub: a starting point for a large subject, not a finished account. Population and tribal figures above are rough, commonly cited ranges, not census data — there has been no reliable, countrywide census of Afghanistan's ethnic composition.

The **Pashtuns** are a people who speak [[Pashto]] as their native language. They live mainly in southern and eastern [[Afghanistan]] and in northwestern Pakistan. The name "Pathan" is an Indian term for the same people, and both are in use.

## Identity and language

Pashto is the core of Pashtun identity, but the group is not defined by language alone. Identity has historically rested on shared descent claims, customary law, and a social code known as *Pashtunwali*. Scholars debate how far these traditions date back and how they have changed.

## Tribal confederacies

Pashtuns are traditionally organised into large tribal confederacies, each dividing further into tribes, clans, and sub-clans — a structure complex enough that it has its own dedicated page.

**See [[Pashtun tribes]]** for the full breakdown, including the [[Durrani]] and [[Ghilzai]] confederacies and the individual tribes within them.

In short: the two confederacies most central to Afghan political history are the Durrani, which produced [[Ahmad Shah Durrani]] and the dynasty that founded the modern Afghan state in 1747, and the Ghilzai, a historic rival based further east.

## Genetics and origins

Claims about the origins of the Pashtuns, including claims about descent from ancient Israelites, are not supported by the evidence and are not repeated here. For what genetic studies can and cannot show about the region, see [[Genetic history of Afghanistan]].
